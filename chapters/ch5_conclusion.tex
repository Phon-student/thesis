\section{SUMMARY OF FINDINGS}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This thesis has presented an end-to-end embodied AI pipeline for a hybrid
bi-pedal/quadrupedal robot that is both controllable through natural language and
internally interpretable through Sparse Autoencoders. The work addressed three
closely related research gaps: the morphological rigidity of existing single-mode
legged robots, the inaccessibility of robot control interfaces to non-technical
users, and the opacity of VLM-based robot controllers.

The principal components of the proposed system are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{A hybrid robot chassis} that switches between bi-pedal and
    quadrupedal locomotion through natural-language commands, built entirely from
    off-the-shelf servo motors and an embedded GPU compute module.

    \item \textbf{A 10,000-record robot conversation dataset} generated automatically
    via a multi-threaded Gemini pipeline, covering single-turn and multi-turn
    task-planning dialogues across 18 action types.

    \item \textbf{A fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B-Thinking model} that maps RGB scene
    images and plain-English instructions to structured \texttt{call\_robot()} tool
    calls, with 96.5\% syntactic validity and 85.0\% semantic correctness on the
    held-out test set, and a median end-to-end latency of 1.73~seconds.

    \item \textbf{Trained TopK Sparse Autoencoder checkpoints} that decompose the
    VLM's layer-20 residual stream into 32,768 interpretable feature dimensions with
    $R^2 > 0.95$ reconstruction quality, and identify Feature~\#5086 as the primary
    task-planning neuron in the agentic model.

    \item \textbf{A feature-steering inference framework} that injects SAE decoder
    vectors at layer~20 to modify robot behaviour at inference time without any model
    retraining, demonstrating a monotone and predictable relationship between steering
    strength and the elicitation of structured coordinate-sequence outputs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{ANSWERS TO RESEARCH QUESTIONS}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Primary Question.}
\textit{How can a compact VLM be fine-tuned to control a hybrid robot through
natural-language commands, and how can Sparse Autoencoders be applied to its
internal activations to produce an interpretable, operator-auditable embodied
AI system?}

The experiments demonstrate that LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL-2B-Thinking on
10,000 automatically generated conversation records is sufficient to produce a
reliable robot controller with an on-device inference latency below the 2-second
target. The SAE analysis confirms that the resulting model's internal representations
contain interpretable features that correlate with specific behavioural patterns,
and that these features can be steered predictably via decoder vector injection.
Together, these results provide a positive and evidence-based answer to the primary
research question.

\paragraph{Secondary Question 1.}
\textit{What dataset composition and generation strategy is most effective?}

A multi-threaded Gemini pipeline that combines single-turn commands (70\%) with
multi-turn planning dialogues (30\%) and enforces the \texttt{call\_robot()} output
format through system-prompt engineering produces a dataset sufficient for reliable
fine-tuning. The inclusion of multi-turn examples is critical for multi-step task
coherence; the experiments show that this aspect of the training distribution most
directly influences multi-turn success rate.

\paragraph{Secondary Question 2.}
\textit{Can a TopK SAE reliably identify monosemantic features corresponding to
interpretable robotic behaviours?}

Manual inspection of 100 randomly sampled features finds that 72\% are interpretably
monosemantic, and the identification of Feature~\#5086 as a task-planning neuron
with strong, consistent activation on multi-step coordinate-sequence inputs confirms
that the SAE methodology produces reliable and meaningful feature decompositions.

\paragraph{Secondary Question 3.}
\textit{Does feature steering produce consistent, predictable changes in robot action
outputs?}

The steering sweep across seven strength levels ($\alpha \in \{0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 80\}$)
confirms a monotone and predictable relationship between steering strength and the
elicitation of structured coordinate outputs, with the optimal operating range
identified as $\alpha \in [10, 20]$. This answers the secondary question affirmatively.

\section{BROADER IMPACT}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The contributions of this thesis have implications beyond the immediate experimental
context.

\textbf{Democratised Robot Control.} The natural-language interface removes the
programming barrier that currently restricts robot operation to trained technicians.
Caregivers, elderly users, and field workers with no technical background can
operate the robot using plain conversational language, broadening the potential
user base for assistive robotics substantially.

\textbf{Interpretable and Auditable AI.} The SAE feature-steering framework provides
operators with a concrete mechanism for auditing the model's internal representations
before deployment. In safety-critical environments --- hospitals, disaster response,
care homes --- the ability to inspect and suppress specific behavioural features is
an important step towards verifiable robot safety, complementing but not replacing
formal verification approaches.

\textbf{Research Contribution to Mechanistic Interpretability.} The present work
provides the first empirical evidence that SAE-based feature steering transfers
successfully from language generation to embodied action generation. This contributes
to the nascent literature on mechanistic interpretability in embodied
AI~\cite{bereska2024mechanistic} and opens a research direction that is currently
largely unexplored.

\textbf{Scalability.} The steering framework is model-agnostic: it requires only
a forward hook on a chosen residual-stream layer and a pre-trained SAE for that
layer. The same technique applies to larger VLMs as compute costs decrease, and to
other embodied AI tasks beyond robot control.


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{LIMITATIONS}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Several limitations of the current work should be acknowledged.

\textbf{Physical Deployment Gap.} The SAE analysis and steering experiments are
conducted on the model's text outputs rather than on a fully closed-loop physical
robot executing trajectories in real time. The transfer of feature-steering effects
to physical hardware execution has not been validated, and it is possible that the
physical robot's sensor noise and actuation errors introduce failure modes that are
not apparent from text-output evaluation.

\textbf{Single-Layer Analysis.} The SAE is trained on layer-20 activations only.
It is possible that important behavioural features are distributed across multiple
layers or reside primarily in other layers of the residual stream. A comprehensive
multi-layer analysis would provide a more complete picture of the model's
representations.

\textbf{Dataset Scale.} With 10,000 training records, the fine-tuning dataset is
relatively small. The high semantic correctness rates observed may not generalise
to out-of-distribution scene types or instruction phrasings not well-represented
in the training data. Scaling to 100,000+ records is identified as a priority for
future work.

\textbf{Qualitative Safety Evaluation.} The safety properties of the steering
mechanism are assessed qualitatively through controlled demonstrations. Formal
verification of safety constraints --- for example, proving that steering a
suppression feature provably prevents a specific class of unsafe actions --- is
beyond the scope of this thesis.


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{FUTURE WORK}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The following directions are identified as the most productive avenues for
extending this work.

\paragraph{Physical Closed-Loop Deployment.}
The most immediate priority is to deploy the full pipeline on the physical robot
with a real-time servo feedback loop, validating that the steering effects observed
in text-output experiments transfer to physical task execution. This will require
integration of the VLM inference loop with the servo control bus at the required
control frequency.

\paragraph{Locomotion Planning with Feature Steering.}
The feature-steering framework has so far been applied to manipulation task planning.
Extending it to locomotion planning --- in particular, identifying SAE features that
correspond to gait-switching decisions and steering these features to trigger or
suppress mode changes --- would demonstrate the framework's applicability to the
robot's full behavioural repertoire.

\paragraph{Dataset Scale-Up.}
Scaling the training dataset to 100,000+ records is expected to improve semantic
correctness on complex multi-step tasks and to extend generalisation to a wider range
of scene types. The automated generation pipeline supports this scaling directly
without additional manual annotation effort.

\paragraph{Harmful Feature Suppression as an Online Safety Filter.}
A natural application of the feature-steering framework is to construct an online
safety filter: a module that identifies activation patterns associated with
potentially harmful or unintended actions (for example, actions towards fragile
objects or toward occupied regions of the workspace) and automatically applies a
negative steering vector to suppress these patterns before the action is executed.
This represents a concrete step towards the ``interpretable safety filter'' vision
articulated in the broader aligned robotics literature~\cite{anthropic2023core}.

\paragraph{Multi-Layer SAE Analysis.}
Training SAEs on all 28 layers of the VLM, or at a minimum on a systematic
sample of layers, would provide a more complete picture of how task-relevant
features develop and interact across the network depth, potentially revealing
feature hierarchies with implications for both interpretability and steering
effectiveness.
