\section{Background and Motivation}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have recently shown strong capability in multimodal
reasoning, instruction following, and human-computer interaction. Free-form language
and visual observations can now be interpreted within a unified representation, so the
gap between human intent and machine response can be reduced.

The next research direction is the migration of this capability from digital tasks to
physical execution. In this context, practical world impact is expected in domains where
natural-language instructions must be converted into reliable actions, such as warehouse
pick-and-place assistance, small-batch manufacturing support, laboratory sample handling,
and assistive service operations in constrained indoor environments.

However, practical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) development is still constrained by
resource requirements. In many published settings, more than 10 hours of robot motion,
specialized data-collection infrastructure, and high-memory GPU hardware are required.
Such requirements are difficult to satisfy for students, independent researchers, and
small university laboratories. Real-robot data collection is costly and time-intensive,
while access to large GPU memory is often limited.

Accordingly, this thesis is motivated by the following question: can an end-to-end VLA
pipeline be developed under a minimal commercial setup, using low-volume data and
moderate compute resources, while retaining usable manipulation performance? To address
this question, a practical framework is developed and evaluated to integrate multimodal
understanding and robot control in a single trainable pipeline for constrained settings.


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Problem Identification}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Based on the motivation above, the core problem is not only model quality but
development accessibility. Existing VLA practices are often constrained by high data
requirements and expensive compute, which slows down experimentation and limits
reproducibility across institutions.

\paragraph{Gap 1: High Entry Cost for VLA Development.}
Many published VLA pipelines depend on long-duration robot data collection
(frequently more than 10 hours per setup), large and diverse task sets, and powerful
multi-GPU infrastructure. This is misaligned with low-cost academic or commercial
prototyping environments.

\paragraph{Gap 2: Insufficient Low-Resource Design Principles.}
There is limited guidance on how to design a VLA pipeline that still works when data
volume is low, task diversity is narrow, and hardware is constrained (e.g., 8 GB VRAM).

\paragraph{Gap 3: Fragmented AI-to-Robot Integration.}
Even when multimodal models perform well, practical deployment fails if communication
between AI outputs and robot controller commands is not standardized, safe, and
composable.

\paragraph{Gap 4: Incomplete Sim-to-Real Validation.}
Evaluation is often focused on simulation only, while real-robot performance,
zero-shot transfer, and deployment safety constraints are underreported.

Building on these identified gaps, this thesis poses the following primary research
question:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can a practical end-to-end Vision-Language-Action pipeline be designed to
train and deploy natural-language robot manipulation under low-data and limited-GPU
conditions while preserving usable performance in both simulation and real-world
settings?}
\end{quote}

The following secondary questions are also addressed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item How should an end-to-end VLA pipeline be structured so that model outputs are
    directly usable for robot execution through a stable AI-to-robot interface?
    \item Which design choices are most effective when dataset duration is around
    one hour and training must fit within an 8 GB VRAM budget?
    \item To what extent can the resulting system follow natural-language commands and
    generalize under zero-shot conditions in simulation and real-world tests?
\end{enumerate}


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Objectives}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The overarching objective of this research is to make VLA development feasible under
commercially accessible resources. The specific objectives are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item To develop an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action pipeline that can be trained
    and deployed with low dataset volume and limited GPU resources.
    \item To build a VLA model that can steer a robot arm according to natural-language
    commands with reliable executable outputs.
\end{enumerate}


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Key Knowledge Resources}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This research requires interdisciplinary knowledge across robotics, mathematics,
machine learning, and systems engineering. The main knowledge resources are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Robot Kinematics}: forward/inverse kinematics, workspace limits,
    and joint-space to task-space mapping for manipulator control.
    \item \textbf{Mathematics}: calculus, probability, and linear algebra for model
    optimization, uncertainty reasoning, and representation learning.
    \item \textbf{Data Science and Data Analysis}: dataset construction, data quality
    auditing, distribution analysis, and experiment interpretation.
    \item \textbf{Modern Machine Learning}: representation learning, sequence modeling,
    multimodal alignment, and training stabilization strategies.
    \item \textbf{Networking and Protocols}: TCP/IP communication for robust,
    low-latency AI-to-robot command transport and runtime synchronization.
\end{enumerate}


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Scope and Limitations}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The scope of this research is delimited as follows. The target platform is a Dobot CR5
robot arm for tabletop manipulation with RGB observations and natural-language
instructions. The dataset scope is intentionally constrained to a low-resource regime
(approximately one hour of robot motion and three task types), and training/deployment
is designed to fit commercial GPU hardware around 8 GB VRAM. The study covers both
simulation and real-world evaluation, including zero-shot setups. Full autonomous
navigation, large-scale multi-robot coordination, and formal safety certification are
outside this scope.


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Contributions}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The principal contributions of this thesis are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{A low-resource end-to-end VLA pipeline} for training and deployment
    under limited dataset and GPU conditions.

    \item \textbf{A practical AI-to-robot communication interface} that converts
    model outputs into composable machine commands over TCP/IP for Dobot control.

    \item \textbf{A natural-language control capability} that enables robot steering
    from user commands without manual per-task scripting.

    \item \textbf{A sim-and-real evaluation framework} covering success rate and
    zero-shot transfer in two domains.

    \item \textbf{An implementation playbook} spanning dataset collection,
    model development, training objective design, inference analysis, and model
    improvement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Organisation}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows.
Chapter~\ref{chap:litreview} reviews related theories and prior work on multimodal
robot learning, natural-language instruction following, and action modeling for
manipulation.
Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} presents the proposed VLA system design, including
module integration (VLM + Vision Expert + Action Expert), design constraints, and
implementation strategy.
Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments} reports experimental results on manipulation tasks,
including instruction-following performance, generalization to unseen tasks, and
real-time latency analysis.
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} summarizes key findings, limitations, and future
directions for general-purpose intelligent robot systems.
