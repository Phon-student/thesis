\section{Background and Motivation}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The aspiration to endow robotic systems with the capacity for autonomous, general-purpose
manipulation has been a defining pursuit of artificial intelligence research for several
decades. Classical approaches to robot control, grounded in handcrafted heuristics,
explicit geometric planning, and task-specific programming pipelines, have achieved
considerable success within narrowly constrained environments. However, these paradigms
exhibit a fundamental brittleness: they demand exhaustive engineering effort for each new
task, fail to gracefully accommodate distributional shifts in the visual environment, and
cannot leverage the rich semantic knowledge embedded in natural language instructions.
Consequently, deploying robotic agents in unstructured, real-world settings---where objects,
lighting conditions, and task goals vary continuously---remains an open and formidable
challenge.

The transformative advances in large-scale deep learning over the past decade have opened
a compelling alternative trajectory. The emergence of Vision-Language Models
(VLMs)~\cite{openai2023gpt4v, liu2023llava}, trained on internet-scale multimodal corpora,
has demonstrated that neural networks can acquire remarkably robust visual understanding,
spatial reasoning, and natural language grounding without task-specific supervision. These
models encode not merely perceptual features, but higher-order semantic relationships---an
ability that maps intuitively onto the requirements of instruction-following robotic
manipulation, where an agent must interpret a command such as
\textit{``put the banana in the green box''}, locate the relevant objects in the scene,
and execute a coordinated sequence of physical motions to accomplish the goal.

This convergence of insights from natural language processing and robotic control has
catalysed the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models---a class of architectures
that unify visual perception, language comprehension, and continuous motor action
generation within a single, end-to-end trainable framework. VLA models have shown
early but encouraging results in generalising manipulation policies to novel objects,
instructions, and scene configurations, suggesting that scaling the paradigms of
foundation model research to the action domain may represent a productive path toward
general-purpose robotic intelligence~\cite{openx2023, oquab2023dinov2}.

Nevertheless, the design of effective VLA architectures presents a set of non-trivial
technical challenges that the existing literature has only partially resolved. Three
interrelated problems are of particular relevance to this thesis. \textit{First}, the
choice of action representation---specifically, how continuous robot trajectories are
encoded into a form that a language backbone can process---critically influences both
the fidelity and the computational tractability of the resulting policy. Discrete
tokenisation strategies, such as the FAST+ tokeniser~\cite{pertsch2025fast}, have emerged
as a strong baseline, yet the design space of customised tokenisation remains
incompletely explored. \textit{Second}, the architectural coupling between the language
backbone and the action generation module---whether through discrete token prediction,
hidden state conditioning, or feature modulation mechanisms such as Feature-wise Linear
Modulation (FiLM)~\cite{perez2018film}---introduces significant tradeoffs between
interpretability, latency, and generalisation capacity. \textit{Third}, the training
methodology, including the formulation of pre-training objectives, the choice of
auxiliary tasks such as object localisation, and the role of masked autoencoding for
action token reconstruction, remains an active area of investigation with no established
consensus on best practice.

These challenges motivate the present work. This thesis proposes and evaluates a novel
VLA architecture designed to address the aforementioned limitations through a principled
synthesis of current best practices and targeted architectural innovations.

\section{Problem Identification}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The central problem addressed in this thesis concerns the effective integration of a
frozen Vision-Language Model backbone with a learned action generation module for
instruction-following robotic manipulation. More specifically, the following
research gaps have been identified through a systematic review of the relevant literature.

\paragraph{Gap 1: Suboptimal Action Representation in Hybrid Architectures.}
Existing VLA frameworks predominantly adopt one of two representational strategies:
purely discrete action tokenisation, wherein trajectory chunks are mapped to a finite
vocabulary and predicted autoregressively, or purely continuous conditioning, wherein
the action model is conditioned on dense hidden states extracted from the language
backbone. Each strategy entails distinct limitations. Discrete tokenisation introduces
quantisation error and constrains temporal resolution, while continuous conditioning
requires maintaining full differentiability through the backbone, imposing substantial
memory overhead and complicating frozen-backbone deployment. A hybrid approach that
selectively combines discrete token prediction with hidden state conditioning based on
the action phase has not been rigorously studied.

\paragraph{Gap 2: Inadequate Exploitation of End-Effector Visual Context.}
Most existing VLA models process visual context through a single shared encoder that
receives a global overview image of the workspace. This design conflates information
at markedly different spatial scales: the coarse, scene-level context necessary for
goal identification and obstacle avoidance, and the fine-grained, proximity-level context
necessary for precise grasping and manipulation. The role of a dedicated end-effector
camera stream, processed through a specialised vision encoder such as
DINOv3~\cite{oquab2023dinov2}, as a complementary input to the action generation module
has received insufficient attention in prior architectural studies.

\paragraph{Gap 3: Unclear Pre-Training Curriculum for Unified VLA Systems.}
The pre-training stage of VLA models typically optimises a next-token prediction
objective over instruction-state-action sequences. However, the optimal composition of
auxiliary objectives---including object localisation prediction, state description
generation, and action token reconstruction via masked autoencoding---and the scheduling
of these objectives across training stages remain poorly understood. In particular, the
extent to which spatial grounding tasks, such as predicting bounding box coordinates
for task-relevant objects, improve downstream manipulation performance through improved
spatial reasoning in the language backbone has not been systematically ablated.

Building upon these identified gaps, this thesis poses the following primary research
question:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can a frozen Vision-Language Model backbone be effectively coupled with
a diffusion-based action expert and a dedicated visual feature extractor to produce
a VLA system that is computationally efficient, data-effective, and capable of
generalising instruction-following manipulation policies to novel objects and
scene configurations?}
\end{quote}

The following secondary questions are also addressed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item What is the relative contribution of discrete action token conditioning
    versus hidden state conditioning on the quality and consistency of generated
    trajectories?
    \item Does incorporating end-effector image features through a specialised encoder
    yield measurable improvements in manipulation precision compared to overview-only
    visual input?
    \item To what extent does including object localisation as an auxiliary pre-training
    task improve the spatial reasoning capacity and downstream task performance of
    the language backbone?
\end{enumerate}


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Objectives}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The overarching objective of this research is to design, implement, and empirically
evaluate a novel VLA architecture that effectively addresses the research gaps
identified in the preceding section. To this end, the following specific objectives
are pursued:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item To propose a dual-encoder VLA architecture comprising a frozen
    VLM backbone (Qwen3 with SigLIP vision encoder, approximately 2~billion parameters),
    a dedicated end-effector vision expert (DINOv3, approximately 86~million parameters),
    and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action expert (approximately 300~million parameters),
    conditioned jointly on hidden states from the VLM and visual features from the
    vision expert.

    \item To design and implement a two-stage pre-training curriculum in which the
    foundation model is trained via next-token prediction over a multi-task sequence
    comprising object localisation coordinates, natural language state descriptions,
    and FAST+ discrete action tokens, while the action expert is trained via
    trajectory reconstruction and masked autoencoding loss.

    \item To conduct ablation studies systematically evaluating the impact of (a)
    discrete versus hidden state action conditioning, (b) end-effector visual feature
    inclusion, and (c) object localisation as an auxiliary pre-training task on
    downstream manipulation performance.

    \item To benchmark the proposed architecture against established VLA baselines,
    including OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla} and GR00T N1~\cite{nvidia2024groot}, across
    a suite of simulated and physical manipulation tasks.
\end{enumerate}


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Scope and Limitations}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To maintain clarity of focus, the scope of this research is delimited as follows.
The proposed architecture targets table-top robotic manipulation tasks involving
a single robotic arm with a parallel-jaw or compliant gripper. The study does not
address bi-manual manipulation, mobile locomotion, or multi-agent coordination.
Visual observations are assumed to be provided by two calibrated RGB cameras: one
providing an overview of the workspace and one mounted at or near the end-effector.
Depth sensing and proprioceptive state estimation are considered supplementary inputs
and are not the primary focus of the architectural investigation. Pre-training data
are drawn from publicly available robot demonstration datasets; data collection
procedures and the construction of novel datasets fall outside the scope of this work.
Furthermore, while inference latency and model compression are acknowledged as
important practical considerations, they are treated as secondary metrics relative
to task success rate and generalisation performance in the evaluation framework.

\section{Contributions}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The principal contributions of this thesis are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{A novel dual-encoder VLA architecture} that decouples the processing
    of overview and end-effector visual streams through a frozen VLM backbone and a
    trainable DINOv3-based vision expert respectively, and conditions a DiT-based
    action expert jointly on VLM hidden states and vision expert features.
    This architectural decomposition is demonstrated to improve manipulation precision
    without incurring proportional increases in computational cost.

    \item \textbf{A structured two-stage pre-training curriculum} incorporating
    object localisation, state description generation, and FAST+ action token
    prediction as complementary next-token prediction tasks for the foundation model,
    together with a reconstruction and masked autoencoding objective for the action
    expert. The curriculum is shown empirically to accelerate convergence and improve
    generalisation relative to single-objective training baselines.

    \item \textbf{A systematic ablation study} characterising the relative
    contributions of the discrete action token pathway and the hidden state
    conditioning pathway to trajectory quality, as well as the incremental benefit
    of end-effector visual features and spatial grounding auxiliary tasks.

    \item \textbf{Open-source release} of the proposed architecture implementation,
    pre-training pipeline, and evaluation benchmarks to facilitate reproducibility
    and further community research.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Organisation}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows. Chapter~\ref{chap:litreview}
provides a comprehensive review of the relevant literature, tracing the development of
VLA models from their foundational precursors in imitation learning and multimodal
language modelling, and critically examining the architectural and training choices
made in seminal works including OpenVLA, GR00T N1, and Diffusion-VLA.
Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} presents the proposed architecture in detail, covering
the design rationale for each component, the mathematical formulation of the training
objectives, and the implementation specifics of the pre-training and fine-tuning
pipelines. Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments} describes the experimental setup, datasets,
evaluation metrics, and baseline comparisons employed in this study, followed by a
thorough presentation and discussion of the empirical results and ablation findings.
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} synthesises the conclusions drawn from the experimental
evidence, revisits the research questions posed in this chapter, acknowledges the
limitations of the current work, and identifies promising directions for future
investigation.
