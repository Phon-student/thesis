\chapter{RELATED THEORIES AND LITERATURE REVIEW}
\label{chap:litreview}

This chapter synthesizes the theoretical and empirical foundations that motivate the
proposed Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework. Rather than surveying prior work in
isolation, the chapter constructs a chronological and structural argument for the 
design choices made in this thesis. 

The discussion is organized into five targeted themes: (1) classical robotic kinematics and the limitations of traditional path planning, (2) the shift toward Fusion-based Vision-Language Models as cognitive engines, (3) the historical evolution of VLA architectures from discrete tokenization to continuous policy experts, (4) the mathematics of continuous Diffusion and Flow Matching, and (5) the current state-of-the-art in VLA integration, including OpenVLA, SmolVLA, and Pi0.5. These themes form the conceptual bridge from problem statement to methodology.

\section{ROBOT KINEMATICS AND CLASSICAL PIPELINES}

Industrial manipulators execute motion in two interconnected mathematical spaces: joint space and Cartesian space. Forward Kinematics (FK) and Inverse Kinematics (IK) form the essential bridge between these spaces. For a 6-Degree-of-Freedom (DOF) collaborative arm such as The Dobot Cr5 (\cref{fig:dobotcr5}), high-level task targets are typically specified in Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z, \mathrm{roll}, \mathrm{pitch}, \mathrm{yaw})$, while physical actuators execute solely joint-level rotational commands $(q_1, \dots, q_6)$.

\subsection{Rigid Body Kinematics and FK/IK}

The configuration of a robot end-effector in three-dimensional space is described by an element of the Special Euclidean group $SE(3) = SO(3) \ltimes \mathbb{R}^3$, where $SO(3)$ is the Lie group of rotation matrices satisfying $R^\top R = I$ and $\det(R) = +1$, and $\mathbb{R}^3$ captures translation. A homogeneous transformation matrix $T \in SE(3)$ takes the block form:
\begin{equation}
    T = \begin{bmatrix} R & p \\ 0^\top & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Forward Kinematics} computes the unique end-effector pose from a known joint configuration $\mathbf{q}$. By mapping the manipulator as a kinematic chain using the Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameterization, the end-effector transform in the base frame is simply the sequential product of local coordinate frame transforms.

Conversely, \textbf{Inverse Kinematics} solves the highly non-linear reverse problem: given a desired end-effector pose $T^* \in SE(3)$, compute the required joint configuration $\mathbf{q}^*$. To prevent massive, unstable joint accelerations near kinematic singularities during execution, numerical IK methods utilizing Damped Least-Squares (DLS) on the manipulator Jacobian matrix $J(\mathbf{q})$ are heavily employed.

\subsection{Traditional Path Planning vs. VLA Cognitive Control}

Historically, once an IK solver identifies a target, moving the arm relies on traditional path planning algorithms such as Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT$^*$) or A$^*$ search. These algorithms operate entirely on geometry: they search for collision-free trajectories through a known mathematical manifold. 

However, traditional path planning suffers from a fundamental ``cognitive bottleneck'': it possesses zero semantic understanding of the world. It requires perfectly mapped 3D collision meshes and static environments. If instructed to "pick up the red apple," a classical planner fails catastrophically because it only understands numeric coordinate vectors, not the concepts of "red" or "apple." Vision-Language-Action models completely bypass this brittle geometric planning. By mapping raw pixels and semantic text directly to motor commands, VLAs eliminate the need for hard-coded collision meshes and explicit state estimation, allowing the robot to interact fluidly with unmapped, unstructured human environments.

\subsection{Practical Hardware Deployment: Dobot CR5 via Python}

To bypass proprietary, inflexible geometric controllers and stream high-frequency AI-generated actions directly to the physical hardware, external compute nodes utilize standard TCP/IP socket programming. The Dobot CR5 provides decoupled ports for motion dispatch and real-time state feedback. Modern VLA pipelines control the arm using Python paradigms similar to the following:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Python TCP/IP control loop for Dobot CR5}]
import time
import struct
import socket

ROBOT_IP = "192.168.1.6"
DASH_PORT, MOV_PORT, FB_PORT = 29999, 30003, 30004
dash = socket.socket(socket.AF_INET, socket.SOCK_STREAM)
dash.connect((ROBOT_IP, DASH_PORT))
move = socket.socket(socket.AF_INET, socket.SOCK_STREAM)
move.connect((ROBOT_IP, MOV_PORT))
feed = socket.socket(socket.AF_INET, socket.SOCK_STREAM)
feed.connect((ROBOT_IP, FB_PORT))

#--- initialise------------------------------------------------------
dash.send(b"EnableRobot()\n"); time.sleep(2)
dash.send(b"ClearError()\n"); time.sleep(0.5)

#--- Cartesian linear move (mm, degrees)-----------------------------
move.send(b"MovL(-300,400,200,-180,0,90,SpeedL=50,AccL=30)\n")

#--- read telemetry--------------------------------------------------
raw = feed.recv(1440)
# one 1440-byte feedback packet
q_actual = struct.unpack_from("6d", raw, offset=24) # joint angles

#--- joint-space move------------------------------------------------
move.send(b"MovJ(0,-30,90,0,60,0,SpeedJ=30,AccJ=20)\n")
time.sleep(3)
dash.send(b"DisableRobot()\n")
for s in (dash, move, feed): s.close()
\end{lstlisting}

By continuously looping {\scriptsize\texttt{ServoP}} or {\scriptsize\texttt{ServoJ}} commands at 10--30 Hz, a VLA policy can dynamically adjust its trajectory in real time based on incoming visual observations.

\section{VISION-LANGUAGE MODELS (VLMs) IN EMBODIED AI}

To interpret raw pixels and free-form human intent, robotic systems embed massive Vision-Language Models. However, the architectural design of these VLMs dictates their feasibility for robotics research.

\subsection{Native vs. Fusion Architectures}

Modern VLMs fall into two primary categories: \textit{Native} and \textit{Fusion} models. Native VLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, Qwen-VL) are trained entirely from scratch on interleaved text-image datasets. While highly capable, training and fine-tuning Native models requires staggering computational resources that are prohibitive for specialized, iterative robotics research.

Conversely, Fusion models (e.g., LLaVA, Prismatic) leverage already-trained unimodal giants. They freeze a powerful Vision Transformer (ViT) and a Large Language Model (LLM), training only a highly lightweight projection layer (such as a multi-layer perceptron or Q-Former) to map visual embeddings into the LLM's input space. 

For robotics, \textbf{Fusion is overwhelmingly the superior paradigm}. Fusion models achieve semantic reasoning and instruction grounding that heavily rival Native models, but at a fraction of the computational cost. Through Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning techniques like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), a 7-Billion parameter Fusion model can be fine-tuned on a single consumer GPU in under 24 hours. Furthermore, Fusion is modular; researchers can seamlessly swap out the visual encoder for one better suited to robotics without retraining the entire language engine.

\subsection{Contrastive Encoders and the Semantic-Spatial Gap}

Fusion VLMs typically utilize visual encoders trained via contrastive alignment, such as CLIP or SigLIP. SigLIP replaces large-batch softmax normalization with a highly efficient sigmoid binary classification loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SigLIP}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \log\sigma\!\left(z_{ij}\left(t\, v_i^\top l_j - b\right)\right)
\end{equation}
While excellent at semantic grounding ("this is a handle"), contrastive encoders inherently discard fine-grained spatial and geometric geometry ("the handle is exactly 4.2cm away"). Standard VLMs suffer from this semantic-spatial gap: they know \textit{what} to do, but lack the millimeter precision of \textit{how} to do it physically.

\section{THE EVOLUTION OF VLA: FROM TOKENS TO EXPERTS}

The integration of VLMs into robotics did not immediately yield smooth, continuous motion. The methodology underwent a rapid evolution from text-prediction to dedicated action policies.

\subsection{The Genesis: Next-Token Prediction}

The concept of a unified Vision-Language-Action model was born with Google DeepMind's RT-1 and RT-2 architectures. Because LLMs operate via autoregressive sequence modeling (predicting the next discrete text word), early researchers framed robot control as a translation task. They discretized the physical joint limits of the robot into text tokens (e.g., {\scriptsize\texttt{<joint\_1\_pos\_15>}}) and simply appended them to the LLM's vocabulary. 

To move the robot, the model would autoregressively generate text strings representing joint coordinates. While revolutionary because it proved large-scale robotic generalization was possible, this paradigm had severe flaws:
1. \textbf{Resolution Floor:} Discretizing continuous 3D space into 256 or 512 text bins mathematically forces a permanent margin of error (typically 2-5mm), making fine dexterous tasks like threading a needle impossible.
2. \textbf{Latency and Error Cascading:} Predicting one joint token at a time is computationally slow. If the model hallucinates or makes a slight error on token 2, the error permanently cascades into the predictions for tokens 3 through 6.

\subsection{The Modern Era: Decoupled Action Policy Experts}

To solve the dexterity and latency issues, the field shifted drastically from next-token prediction to \textbf{Action Policy Experts}. Instead of forcing the LLM to output discrete robot coordinates, the VLM is used purely as a cognitive backbone. It processes the image and text, then outputs a dense, highly semantic continuous embedding (a "thought vector"). 

This embedding is passed into a separate, lightweight Action Expert network (such as a Diffusion model). The Action Expert operates entirely in continuous space, bypassing discrete tokenization. This hybrid approach flawlessly merges the internet-scale semantic reasoning of the VLM with the high-frequency, continuous precision of classical control.

\section{DIFFUSION MODELS IN ROBOTICS CONTROL}

To serve as the continuous Action Expert, Diffusion Models have become the gold standard, replacing traditional Behavioral Cloning (BC). Standard BC simply averages expert demonstrations, failing catastrophically when presented with multimodal human data (e.g., reaching an object from the left vs. the right).

\subsection{DDPM and Action Chunking}

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) gradually corrupt a target action chunk (a sequence of future actions) with Gaussian noise, and train a neural network $\epsilon_\theta$ to predict the added noise, conditioned on the VLM's observation embeddings $c$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DDPM}} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_0,\,\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I),\,t} \!\left[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, c)\|_2^2\right]
\end{equation}
This iterative denoising mirrors physical trajectory refinement: starting from random noise and progressively carving out a specific, safe physical trajectory. Predicting an entire "Action Chunk" (e.g., 50 future steps at once) rather than a single step provides immense temporal smoothness and mitigates inference latency.

\subsection{U-Net vs. DiT Backbones}

Early diffusion policies utilized 1D temporal convolutional \textbf{U-Net} architectures. While exceptionally sample-efficient on small robotic datasets, U-Nets struggle to process the massive, high-dimensional context tokens generated by modern VLMs. 

Consequently, the field adopted Diffusion Transformers (DiT). DiT treats the noisy physical action chunks and the VLM's semantic tokens as a single, unified sequence, relying entirely on scalable self-attention mechanisms. This drastically improves the model's ability to execute complex, multi-stage reasoning tasks over long horizons (\cref{fig:unet-dit}).

\subsection{Flow Matching for Fast Inference}

Despite advancements like DDIM sampling, the iterative nature of diffusion incurs latency. Flow Matching has recently emerged to solve this. Instead of iterative step-by-step denoising, Flow Matching learns a continuous vector field that transports pure noise directly to the expert action distribution along straight-line paths:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1} \!\left[\|v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - (\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0)\|_2^2\right]
\end{equation}
Because straight lines possess zero curvature, Flow Matching requires vastly fewer integration steps (often $\leq 10$), perfectly satisfying the strict sub-100ms latency constraints of real-world robotic deployment.

\section{STATE-OF-THE-ART IN VLA INTEGRATION}

Modern SOTA models synthesize these concepts into highly capable foundation models for robotics.

\subsection{OpenVLA}
OpenVLA is the open-source pioneer of large-scale VLA control. Built on a 7B-parameter Llama-2 fusion backbone, it combines semantic SigLIP features with spatially dense DINOv2 features (\cref{fig:openvla}). Fine-tuned efficiently via LoRA on massive cross-embodiment datasets (nearly 1 million trajectories), OpenVLA provides unmatched out-of-the-box generalization. However, because it relies on the older paradigm of autoregressive discrete token prediction (256 quantization bins), it remains structurally bottlenecked in tasks requiring sub-millimeter precision or rapid 30Hz continuous adjustments.

\subsection{SmolVLA}
Addressing the massive computational overhead of 7B+ parameter models like OpenVLA, \textbf{SmolVLA} (\cref{fig:smolvla}) represents the cutting-edge shift toward highly compressed, edge-efficient architectures. By leveraging aggressively distilled LLM backbones (typically $\leq$ 2B parameters) and heavily optimized multimodal connectors, SmolVLA significantly reduces VRAM footprint and inference latency. It proves that with targeted, high-quality demonstration data and optimized parameter footprints, VLAs can be deployed directly onto edge compute (e.g., localized robot processing units) without losing core semantic reasoning capabilities, making it ideal for cost-constrained academic and industrial applications.

\subsection{Pi-Zero (\texorpdfstring{$\pi_0$}{pi0}) and Pi0.5}
Developed by Physical Intelligence, $\pi_0$ and the refined Pi0.5 (\cref{fig:pi05}) represent the pinnacle of continuous VLA control. Breaking away from discrete tokens completely, Pi0.5 employs the decoupled Action Policy Expert paradigm. It utilizes a powerful Fusion VLM (PaliGemma) to process multimodal context, piping those rich embeddings directly into a DiT-based continuous Flow Matching expert. This perfectly merges the internet-scale cognitive reasoning of a VLM with the smooth, high-frequency, millimeter-precision capabilities of modern diffusion models, resulting in unprecedented success rates on highly dexterous bimanual manipulation tasks.

\section{GENERALIZATION AND CHAPTER SUMMARY}

Generalization remains the ultimate challenge in embodied AI. Physical robot demonstrations are profoundly expensive to harvest, requiring algorithms to infer broad, robust behaviors from limited data. The literature dictates that generalizable transfer requires robust decoupling of semantic and spatial architectures, cross-embodiment pretraining, and highly stable continuous action interfaces.

In summary, classical geometric pipelines fail due to extreme brittleness, lacking the cognitive capacity to understand human intent. The field evolved to adopt Fusion VLMs, which provide unparalleled semantic reasoning at low computational costs. While early systems like RT-1 and OpenVLA framed control as discrete next-token prediction, the field rapidly advanced to continuous DiT and Flow Matching Action Experts, as seen in Pi0.5. To address the need for robust, precise manipulation on accessible hardware budgets, the methodology proposed in this thesis heavily leverages these findings: uniting a parameter-efficient Fusion VLM backbone with a continuous, latency-optimized Diffusion expert for real-world control of the Dobot CR5.
