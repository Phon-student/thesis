\chapter{EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter evaluates the performance of the proposed Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework. The evaluation validates the architectural improvements detailed in Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}, specifically testing memory efficiency, zero-shot spatial generalization, and sim-to-real transfer capabilities. Furthermore, qualitative trajectory analysis, ablation studies, and failure mode analyses are presented to critically discuss the system's physical constraints.

%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Evaluation Setup and Hardware Inference}
\label{sec:eval_setup}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A primary objective of this research was to deploy a continuous VLA policy on accessible, commercial-grade hardware without exceeding an 8~GB VRAM budget. The trained model was deployed on a local workstation utilizing an NVIDIA RTX 5070 GPU. 

During live inference, the framework successfully operated well within this constraint, consuming only \textbf{4~GB of VRAM}. The control loop achieved real-time execution utilizing an action-chunking paradigm: the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) predicts a future horizon of 30 steps. As the physical Dobot CR5 executes these 30 sub-actions, the model instantly predicts the next 30-step chunk in the background. This receding-horizon approach completely masks the diffusion inference latency, resulting in fluid, uninterrupted robotic motion without the stuttering commonly associated with iterative generative models.

\section{Discussion: Drivers of Accuracy and Baseline Comparison}
\label{sec:accuracy_discussion}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The empirical results demonstrate a substantial performance gap between the standard VLA baseline and the proposed framework (a 175\% relative improvement in simulation and a 30\% absolute improvement in real-world deployment). This performance delta is not the result of merely scaling up model parameters, but rather the direct consequence of three targeted architectural interventions that address the fundamental bottlenecks of standard Diffusion Transformer policies.

\subsection{Prevention of Modality Collapse via Structured Masking}
Standard VLA baselines typically pass visual and text embeddings into the action expert via dense, unconstrained cross-attention. In highly complex scenes, this often leads to \textit{modality collapse}, where the model over-attends to dominant visual features (e.g., a brightly colored distractor object) and effectively ignores the semantic text instruction or the local wrist-camera perspective. 

The proposed model achieves higher accuracy because the \textbf{Structured Latent Conditioning} structurally prevents this collapse. By forcing the 64 latent tokens through a strict boolean attention mask, the architecture guarantees that the DiT receives an explicitly disentangled state vector. 10 tokens are mathematically forced to retain the language instruction, while 15 tokens strictly monitor the end-effector. This ensures that semantic intent and localized spatial precision are never overwhelmed by global visual noise.

\subsection{Forced Spatial Extrapolation via Overview-Aware Dropout}
A primary reason the baseline model achieved only 20\% zero-shot success in simulation is its susceptibility to "wrist-camera cheating." Standard policies often learn to execute reaches by continuously centering the target in the end-effector camera. Consequently, if an unseen target is placed outside the initial wrist-camera Field of View (FOV), the baseline policy panics and fails to initiate movement.

The proposed framework's superior zero-shot generalization (55\%) is driven by the \textbf{Overview-Aware Strategy}. By applying a 20\% dropout to the end-effector camera during training, the model was mathematically forced to learn global coordinate mapping from the overview camera. During inference, even if the target is outside the wrist FOV, the model successfully relies on the overview tokens to extrapolate the initial reaching trajectory.

\subsection{Gradient Efficiency via Kinematic Weighting}
Finally, the baseline model attempts to optimize the Flow Matching objective equally across all 6 degrees of freedom. In tasks where specific joints are idle, the baseline wastes computational capacity attempting to model the micro-vibrations and hardware noise of those idle joints, leading to compounding trajectory drift.

The proposed model significantly improved physical success rates by introducing the \textbf{Weighted Position Loss}. By applying a 2.0 penalty to moving joints and relaxing the penalty on stationary joints (0.8), the training gradients were explicitly focused on the joints driving the task. This resulted in significantly higher precision at the end-effector, directly translating to the 30\% success rate in physical sim-to-real transfer where millimeter-level accuracy is strictly required.

\section{Ablation Study: The Necessity of Weighted Position Loss}
\label{sec:ablation_loss}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To empirically isolate the impact of the precomputed kinematic motion masks, an ablation study was conducted. The model was retrained with standard, unweighted Position Loss (applying a uniform weight of 1.0 to all joints, regardless of their kinematic status). 

When deployed, the unweighted model's success rate dropped to \textbf{0\%}. Without dynamic weighting, the Flow Matching objective over-indexes on reconstructing the hardware noise of stationary joints, drastically distracting the gradient updates from the joints actually driving the end-effector. This 0\% ablation result conclusively proves that dynamic loss scaling is a fundamental requirement for stable continuous control in low-data regimes.

\section{Failure Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:failure_modes}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Analyzing the 45\% failure rate in simulation and the 70\% failure rate in the real world reveals two primary modalities of limitation within the current architecture:

\subsection{Extreme Spatial Variance (Out-of-Distribution Kinematics)}
While the model generalized well to minor shifts in object placement, it failed consistently when target objects were placed at the extreme edges of the Dobot CR5's reachable workspace. If the new initial position deviated too radically from the distributions seen in the training data, the Flow Matching vector field could not confidently map the noise to an expert trajectory, resulting in the end-effector stopping short of the target.

\subsection{Visual Domain Shift (Semantic Confusion)}
During real-world testing, it became evident that the model possesses virtually no Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) visual awareness. When entirely novel object configurations, lighting conditions, or reflective materials were introduced to the physical scene, the VLM backbone struggled to comprehend the visual input. In these instances, the structured latent tokens completely failed to align the text instruction with the new spatial features, causing the visual conditioning to collapse.

This phenomenon is explicitly visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:attention-map-error}, which maps the per-token cross-attention weights during a real-world task attempting to follow the instruction: "\textit{Move up to the blue box from the yellow box}." 

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{images/trajectory_error.png}
    \caption[Attention map scattering during semantic confusion]{Per-token cross-attention heatmaps during a visual domain shift failure. The scattered, random activations across the background and robot arm demonstrate a complete breakdown in spatial-semantic grounding for OOD objects.}
    \label{fig:attention-map-error}
\end{figure}

If the model possessed robust OOD visual awareness, the heatmaps for the tokens "blue", "yellow", and "box" would show highly localized, concentrated activations directly over the target objects. Instead, the visualizations reveal highly erratic and random attention distributions. The DiT's spatial focus bleeds heavily into the background environment, the table, and the manipulator arm itself. 

This scattered attention map proves that under severe visual domain shifts, the model's semantic grounding completely breaks down. Because the VLM cannot visually isolate the OOD objects, the Action Expert receives highly unstructured, random latent context, resulting in the robot idling or executing completely incorrect trajectories.
