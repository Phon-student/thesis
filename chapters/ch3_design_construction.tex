\chapter{RESEARCH METHODOLOGY AND SYSTEM DESIGN}
\label{chap:methodology}

This chapter presents the complete design and construction of the proposed
Vision-Language-Action architecture. It details the rationale for each
architectural component, the mathematical formulation of the training objectives,
the pre-training curriculum, and the implementation specifications. The presentation
follows the sequence of the system's information flow: from raw sensor inputs through
the foundation model and vision expert, to the action expert and the final continuous
trajectory output.


%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{RESEARCH METHODOLOGY OVERVIEW}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The research methodology adopted in this thesis is structured around three
sequential phases: architecture design, pre-training, and empirical evaluation.
In the design phase, the system components are specified and their interactions
formalised. In the pre-training phase, the foundation model and action expert are
trained on large-scale robot demonstration datasets using the multi-task objectives
described in Section~\ref{sec:pretraining}. In the evaluation phase, the trained
system is assessed on a suite of manipulation tasks under controlled conditions,
with systematic ablation experiments isolating the contribution of each architectural
and training choice. This chapter covers the first two phases; the evaluation phase
is detailed in Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments}.

The methodology is grounded in the following core design principles:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Frozen Backbone.} The VLM backbone is kept frozen throughout
    training to preserve the broadly generalisable visual and linguistic representations
    acquired during foundation model pre-training, in accordance with the findings
    of~\cite{pertsch2025kilovla}.

    \item \textbf{Separation of Scales.} Overview-level semantic understanding
    and end-effector-level spatial precision are processed through separate,
    specialised encoders rather than a single shared encoder, enabling each module
    to be optimised for its respective representational requirement.

    \item \textbf{Discrete-Continuous Hybrid Conditioning.} The action expert
    receives conditioning signals from both the discrete FAST+ action token pathway
    and the continuous hidden state pathway of the VLM backbone, combining the
    complementary strengths of both representations.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Task Pre-Training.} The foundation model is trained on a
    curriculum that jointly optimises for object localisation, state description
    generation, and action token prediction, promoting spatial reasoning alongside
    action grounding.
\end{enumerate}

\section{SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Overall Architecture}

The proposed system comprises three principal components: (1) a frozen VLM backbone
responsible for high-level scene understanding and discrete action token prediction,
(2) a trainable DINOv3-based Vision Expert that extracts fine-grained spatial features
from the end-effector camera stream, and (3) a trainable Diffusion Transformer (DiT)
Action Expert that generates continuous kinematic trajectories conditioned on the
outputs of the two preceding modules. Figure~\ref{fig:overall_arch} illustrates the
overall architecture and the information flow between components at inference timestep
$t$.

             image; the Action Expert (purple) generates the continuous trajectory
             conditioned on both upstream modules.}
    \label{fig:overall_arch}
\end{figure}

Formally, at each timestep $t$, the system receives the following inputs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A natural language instruction $\mathcal{I}$
          (e.g., \textit{``put the banana in the green box''})
    \item An overview RGB image $I^{\text{ov}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$
          of the workspace
    \item An end-effector RGB image $I^{\text{ef}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H' \times W' \times 3}$
    \item A textual state description $s_t$
          (e.g., \textit{``move to the banana on the bottom right of the table''})
\end{itemize}

and produces as output a continuous trajectory chunk
$\hat{\tau}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times A}$, where $N$ is the number of prediction
timesteps in the chunk and $A$ is the dimensionality of the robot's action space.

\subsection{VLM Backbone}
\label{sec:vlm}

The VLM backbone is implemented as a Qwen3 autoregressive language model augmented
with a SigLIP vision encoder, with a combined parameter count of approximately
2~billion. The SigLIP encoder maps the overview image $I^{\text{ov}}_t$ into a sequence
of patch embeddings $\mathbf{V}^{\text{ov}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L_v \times d_v}$, where
$L_v$ is the number of patches and $d_v$ is the visual embedding dimension. A learned
linear projector $W_{\text{proj}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v \times d_m}$ maps these embeddings
into the language model's token embedding space of dimension $d_m$.

The language model then processes the concatenated token sequence:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_t =
    \bigl[\,\text{tok}(\mathcal{I})\,,\;
           W_{\text{proj}}\,\mathbf{V}^{\text{ov}}\,,\;
           \text{tok}(s_t)\,,\;
           \mathbf{a}^{1:k-1}_t\,\bigr]
    \label{eq:vlm_input}
\end{equation}

where $\text{tok}(\cdot)$ denotes the tokenised embedding of a text string and
$\mathbf{a}^{1:k-1}_t$ are the previously predicted FAST+ action tokens at the
current timestep. The backbone produces two outputs that are consumed by downstream
modules:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item \textbf{Discrete Action Tokens} $\hat{\mathbf{a}}_t \in \mathcal{V}^K$:
    the next FAST+ token is predicted autoregressively at each decoding step via a
    softmax output head over the action vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$.

    \item \textbf{Hidden States} $\mathbf{H}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L_x \times d_m}$:
    the sequence of hidden state vectors from the final Transformer layer, encoding
    the model's joint contextual representation of the instruction, visual scene,
    and current state.
\end{enumerate}

The backbone weights are frozen throughout all training stages. A lightweight
LoRA adapter~\cite{hu2022lora} may optionally be applied to the final self-attention
layers corresponding to the \texttt{<start-of-action>} and \texttt{<end-of-action>}
special tokens, enabling the backbone to learn task-specific action boundary
representations without perturbing its general representations.

\subsection{Vision Expert}
\label{sec:vision_expert}

The Vision Expert is implemented as a DINOv3 vision Transformer of approximately
86~million parameters. It receives the end-effector image $I^{\text{ef}}_t$ and
produces a sequence of patch-level feature embeddings:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t = \text{DINOv3}(I^{\text{ef}}_t)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{L_p \times d_p}
    \label{eq:dino_features}
\end{equation}

where $L_p$ is the number of patches and $d_p$ is the DINOv3 feature dimension.
Because DINOv3 is pre-trained via self-supervised patch-level distillation without
semantic label supervision, its patch features encode dense spatial geometry and
texture information at fine resolution, providing the action expert with precise
positional cues about the immediate manipulation environment that are absent from
the VLM backbone's higher-level semantic representations. The Vision Expert is
trainable and is optimised jointly with the Action Expert during pre-training.

\subsection{Action Expert}
\label{sec:action_expert}

The Action Expert is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) of approximately 300~million
parameters that generates continuous trajectory chunks via a flow-matching objective.
At each denoising step $i$ of the generation process, the DiT receives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Noisy trajectory $\tilde{\tau}^{(i)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times A}$
          sampled from the current noise schedule
    \item Hidden states $\mathbf{H}_t$ from the VLM backbone, incorporated via
          cross-attention layers
    \item End-effector features $\mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t$ from the Vision Expert,
          also incorporated via cross-attention layers
    \item Denoising timestep embedding $\phi(i)$
\end{itemize}

The DiT predicts the vector field $v_\theta(\tilde{\tau}^{(i)}_t, i)$ that transports
the noisy sample toward the clean trajectory distribution:

\begin{equation}
    \hat{\tau}_t = \tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_t +
    \int_0^1 v_\theta\!\left(\tilde{\tau}^{(i)}_t,\, i\right) di
    \label{eq:flow_trajectory}
\end{equation}

where the integral is approximated using an ODE solver with a fixed number of
function evaluations. The cross-attention over $\mathbf{H}_t$ provides the primary
semantic conditioning signal, while the cross-attention over $\mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t$
provides the spatial precision signal. These two conditioning streams are processed
through separate cross-attention sublayers within each DiT block, with their outputs
summed before the subsequent feed-forward sublayer.

\subsection{State Representation}
\label{sec:state}

Following the best practice identified in the literature review, the robot state
$s_t$ is represented as a \textbf{Text State}: a natural language description of the
current manipulation sub-goal (e.g., \textit{``move to a banana on the bottom right
of the table''}). This representation is incorporated into the VLM input
sequence~(\ref{eq:vlm_input}) as a standard text token sequence, processed by the
frozen backbone without any architectural modification. This contrasts with the
Continuous State representation employed by GR00T N1~\cite{nvidia2024groot}, which
requires a dedicated FiLM injection pathway and additional trainable parameters at
the backbone interface. The Text State is preferred here because it is natively
compatible with the backbone's pre-trained token processing mechanism and imposes
no structural modification on the frozen model.

\section{ACTION TOKENISATION}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{FAST+ Discrete Tokenisation}

Continuous trajectory chunks $\tau_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times A}$ are tokenised
into a sequence of discrete tokens $\mathbf{a}_t \in \mathcal{V}^K$ using the
FAST+ tokeniser~\cite{pertsch2025fast}. The tokenisation proceeds in three stages:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Frequency Transformation.} A Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)
    is applied along the temporal dimension of the trajectory chunk, yielding
    a frequency-domain representation $\hat{\tau}_t = \text{DCT}(\tau_t)$.
    The energy of smooth robot trajectories is concentrated in the low-frequency
    DCT coefficients, enabling the trajectory to be compactly represented by
    retaining only the dominant $K'$ coefficients.

    \item \textbf{Scalar Quantisation.} Each retained DCT coefficient is
    independently quantised to the nearest centroid in a learned scalar codebook
    $\mathcal{C} \subset \mathbb{R}$, producing a sequence of $K' \times A$
    discrete indices that are flattened and mapped to the action vocabulary
    $\mathcal{V}$ via a bijective mapping.

    \item \textbf{Detokenisation.} At inference, the predicted discrete token
    sequence is decoded back to DCT coefficients, the discarded high-frequency
    components are set to zero, and an inverse DCT is applied to recover the
    continuous trajectory $\hat{\tau}_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The FAST+ tokeniser is pre-fitted on the training dataset before any model
training begins, and its parameters are held fixed throughout all subsequent
training stages.

\subsection{Special Action Boundary Tokens}

Two special tokens, \texttt{<start-of-action>} and \texttt{<end-of-action>},
are added to the VLM vocabulary to demarcate the boundary of the action token
sequence within the autoregressive generation process. The optional LoRA adapter
is applied exclusively to the attention layers that process these boundary tokens,
ensuring that the backbone learns appropriate action-transition representations
while its general representations remain unperturbed.

\section{PRE-TRAINING CURRICULUM}
\label{sec:pretraining}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The pre-training stage trains the two trainable components---the Vision Expert and
the Action Expert---using a two-track curriculum applied to the same demonstration
dataset. The frozen VLM backbone participates in the forward pass but receives no
gradient updates.

\subsection{Foundation Model Track: Next-Token Prediction}

The VLM backbone processes sequences of the following multi-task form:

\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\mathcal{I}}_{\text{instruction}}
    \;\rightarrow\;
    \underbrace{\texttt{banana \;<loc12>\;<loc25>\;...}}_{\text{[1]\;object localisation}}
    \;\rightarrow\;
    \underbrace{s_t}_{\text{[2]\;state description}}
    \;\rightarrow\;
    \underbrace{\mathbf{a}_t \in \mathcal{V}^K}_{\text{[3]\;FAST+ action tokens}}
    \label{eq:ntp_sequence}
\end{equation}

This multi-task sequence trains the backbone to jointly predict three categories
of output tokens at each timestep:

\begin{enumerate}[label={[\arabic*]}]
    \item \textbf{Object Localisation.} The backbone is trained to predict the
    bounding box coordinates of task-relevant objects encoded as \texttt{<loc>}
    tokens indexing a discretised coordinate grid. This auxiliary task is motivated
    by the finding of~\cite{chen2024localization} that spatial grounding training
    measurably improves downstream spatial reasoning capacity.

    \item \textbf{State Description.} The backbone is trained to generate the
    current natural language state $s_t$, grounding the language model in the
    temporal progression of the manipulation task across successive timesteps.

    \item \textbf{FAST+ Action Tokens.} The backbone is trained to predict the
    FAST+ discrete action token sequence $\mathbf{a}_t$ corresponding to the
    current trajectory chunk.
\end{enumerate}

The training objective for this track is the standard cross-entropy loss:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}} =
    -\sum_{j} \log p_\theta\!\left(y_j \;\big|\; y_{<j},\, \mathbf{X}_t\right)
    \label{eq:ntp_loss}
\end{equation}

where $y_j$ denotes the $j$-th target token in sequence~(\ref{eq:ntp_sequence}).

\subsection{Action Expert Track: Reconstruction and Masked Autoencoding}

In parallel, the Action Expert and Vision Expert are trained on a trajectory
reconstruction objective. Given the ground-truth trajectory $\tau_t$, a noisy
version is constructed by interpolating linearly between the trajectory and a
Gaussian noise sample at a random noise level $i \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$:

\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\tau}^{(i)}_t = (1 - i)\,\tau_t + i\,\varepsilon,
    \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
    \label{eq:flow_interpolation}
\end{equation}

The Action Expert is trained to predict the conditional vector field that
transports the noisy sample back toward the clean trajectory:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} =
    \mathbb{E}_{\tau_t,\,\varepsilon,\,i}\!\left[
        \left\| v_\theta\!\left(\tilde{\tau}^{(i)}_t,\, i
        \;\Big|\; \mathbf{H}_t,\, \mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t\right)
        - (\tau_t - \varepsilon) \right\|^2
    \right]
    \label{eq:flow_loss}
\end{equation}

An additional Masked Autoencoding (MAE) loss is applied to the FAST+ discrete
action token sequence, wherein a random subset $\mathcal{M}$ of action tokens is
masked and the Action Expert must reconstruct the masked tokens from the remaining
context and the conditioning signals:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}} =
    -\sum_{k \in \mathcal{M}}
    \log p_\theta\!\left(a_k \;\big|\;
    \mathbf{a}_{\setminus\mathcal{M}},\,
    \mathbf{H}_t,\,
    \mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t\right)
    \label{eq:mae_loss}
\end{equation}

The MAE objective encourages the Action Expert to develop a richer internal
representation of action sequence structure, improving robustness to partial
or noisy action token conditioning at inference time.

The total training objective is the weighted sum of the three losses:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}}
    + \lambda_{\text{FM}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}
    + \lambda_{\text{MAE}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}

where $\lambda_{\text{FM}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{MAE}}$ are scalar weighting
hyperparameters determined by grid search on the validation split.

\section{DATASET AND DATA PROCESSING}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Training Dataset}

Pre-training data are drawn from the Open X-Embodiment dataset~\cite{openx2023},
a large-scale collection of robot demonstration trajectories aggregated from
multiple robotic platforms and research institutions. Demonstrations are filtered
to retain only single-arm tabletop manipulation episodes recorded with at least
one wrist-mounted or end-effector-adjacent camera, yielding a subset compatible
with the dual-camera input format of the proposed architecture. Each episode is
segmented into fixed-length trajectory chunks of $N = 16$ timesteps, with a
stride of $N/2 = 8$ timesteps to produce overlapping chunks that increase the
effective dataset size.

\subsection{State Description Annotation}

Natural language state descriptions $s_t$ are generated automatically for each
trajectory chunk by querying a separate, larger VLM with the overview image and
the instruction as context. The generated descriptions are manually validated on
a held-out subset of 500 episodes to confirm that they correctly reflect the
sub-goal of each chunk before being incorporated into the training sequences.

\subsection{Localisation Label Generation}

Bounding box labels for task-relevant objects are generated using a zero-shot
open-vocabulary detector applied to each overview image. Detected boxes are
discretised onto a $256 \times 256$ coordinate grid to produce the
\texttt{<loc>} token sequences used in the localisation pre-training sub-task.

\section{IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Hardware and Software}

All training experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A100 80~GB GPUs. The VLM backbone
is loaded in \texttt{bfloat16} precision with gradient checkpointing enabled to
reduce memory overhead. The Vision Expert and Action Expert are trained in
\texttt{float32} precision. The codebase is implemented in Python using the
PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} deep learning framework, with the Hugging Face
Transformers library~\cite{wolf2020transformers} used for backbone weight management.

\subsection{Optimiser and Learning Rate Schedule}

Trainable parameters are optimised using AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with a
peak learning rate of $3 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay of $10^{-2}$, and gradient
clipping at norm 1.0. A cosine annealing schedule with a linear warmup over the
first 2\% of training steps is applied. The optional LoRA adapter uses a separate
learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and rank $r = 16$.

\section{INFERENCE PROCEDURE}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

At inference time, the system operates in a receding-horizon fashion. At each
control timestep $t$, the VLM backbone performs a single autoregressive forward
pass over the input context~(\ref{eq:vlm_input}), producing hidden states
$\mathbf{H}_t$ and the discrete action token sequence $\hat{\mathbf{a}}_t$.
Simultaneously, the Vision Expert processes the current end-effector image to
produce $\mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t$. The Action Expert then denoises a Gaussian
noise sample $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I^{N \times A})$ conditioned on
$\mathbf{H}_t$ and $\mathbf{F}^{\text{ef}}_t$ over 10 ODE integration steps,
yielding the predicted trajectory chunk $\hat{\tau}_t$. The first $N/2$ steps
of $\hat{\tau}_t$ are executed on the robot; the remaining steps are discarded,
and the process repeats at the next timestep with updated observations. This
receding-horizon execution strategy balances trajectory smoothness with
reactivity to environmental changes.
